01 / Purpose

Forecast tomorrow without pretending the atmosphere is simple.

The project asks a practical regression question: given Lahore’s recent PM2.5 behaviour and current weather, what concentration should be expected the next day?

Daily StateAir observations from OpenAQ are aligned with temperature, relative humidity, wind speed and precipitation from NASA POWER. Recent pollution history, rolling statistics, weather changes and seasonal cycles become inputs to a model that predicts the next-day change in PM2.5.

Modelling principle Respect time order, compare every model with a strong persistence baseline, and leave long data gaps unfilled.

02 / Questions

Four questions shape the investigation.

  1. 01

    How complete and reliable is the Lahore PM2.5 record?

  2. 02

    Do weather signals improve a recent-value forecast?

  3. 03

    Does predicting daily change generalise across time?

  4. 04

    Where does the model still fail—especially during abrupt spikes?

03 / Method

A time-aware route from APIs to a tested forecast.

Each stage protects the next from leakage, invented values or overly optimistic evaluation.

  1. 01Acquire

    Collect Lahore PM2.5 and local weather by date.

  2. 02Audit

    Reindex the calendar and measure missing periods.

  3. 03Engineer

    Create lags, rolling signals, momentum and seasonality.

  4. 04Validate

    Use five expanding chronological train–test folds.

  5. 05Compare

    Require improvement over tomorrow-equals-today.

04 / Data quality

Short gaps were repaired; long gaps stayed visible.

The calendar audit found 351 missing PM2.5 days after aligning the full period. Linear interpolation was restricted to complete gaps of three days or fewer, filling 46 values and leaving 305 unavailable.

  • 2,038 daily PM2.5 records received
  • 75 calendar dates absent from the response
  • 47 days in the longest gap
  • 14 gaps longer than seven days

Decision: exclude remaining gaps from modelling rather than manufacture pollution trajectories across long periods.

06 / Findings

What the evidence supports.

The useful result is not a single score; it is a pattern that persists across chronological tests.

01

Change modelling generalised.

The enhanced model beat persistence in all five folds, with improvements from 3.74% to 9.92%.

Average improvement · 6.54%
02

Recent exposure dominated.

The three-day mean and current PM2.5 carried about 70.7% of earlier Random Forest importance.

Predictive importance · not causal attribution
03

Weather added context.

Temperature, wind, humidity and precipitation contributed about 16.7% of the earlier importance.

04

Sudden spikes remain hard.

07 / Statistical perspective

A forecast is conditional evidence, not an air-quality guarantee.

MAE summarises typical absolute error, but it does not cap the error on a severe day. The held-out folds show that performance changes through time, and the spike diagnostic shows why operational warnings need extra safeguards.

09 / Boundaries

What this project does not claim.

Spatial coverage

One Lahore monitor cannot represent every neighbourhood or all of Pakistan.

Data gaps

Long missing periods reduce training coverage and may conceal unusual episodes.

Emission drivers

Traffic, industry, crop burning and dust-event variables are not directly included.

Operations

The saved model is a research artefact, not a live health-warning service.

Recommended next phase

Add multiple Lahore monitors, satellite aerosol information, boundary-layer and wind-direction variables, event indicators and a live ingestion pipeline—then test prediction intervals and spike-sensitive losses.
