01 / Purpose

From raw transactions to evidence that can be questioned.

The aim was not to decorate a dataset with charts. It was to build a traceable analysis in which every result follows from a stated decision.

The project examines 281 retail transaction records across one month. It validates the data, handles missing sales transparently, studies the distribution of transaction values, tracks daily activity and measures customer contribution.

Analytical principle Report what the data supports—and make the boundaries of that evidence visible.

02 / Questions

Six questions guided the analysis.

  1. 01

    Is the dataset complete, internally consistent and suitable for analysis?

  2. 02

    What do the centre, spread and shape of transaction values reveal?

  3. 03

    How do daily sales and transaction volume change across the month?

  4. 04

    Which observed customers contribute the most recorded sales?

  5. 05

    How is daily sales performance associated with transaction volume?

  6. 06

    Which conclusions are supported—and which would overstate the evidence?

04 / Data quality

Missing revenue was excluded—not invented.

Four transactions have no sales amount. They are excluded from revenue calculations rather than filled with the mean, because imputation would manufacture sales that were never observed.

  • 0 duplicate rows
  • 0 duplicate transaction IDs
  • 0 missing customer IDs
  • 4 missing sales values

Decision: preserve the raw file, analyse complete sales records, and report the exclusion explicitly.

06 / Findings

What the observed month reveals.

Each finding is paired with the qualification needed to interpret it responsibly.

01

A right-skewed transaction distribution.

The mean transaction value is 56.13, while the median is 36.28. A smaller group of high-value purchases pulls the mean upward, so the median better represents a typical transaction.

IQR upper fence185.366 high-value observations · 7.6% of sales

Interpretation: the six observations were retained because no available field indicates that they are data-entry errors.

02

Daily performance varies substantially.

Recorded daily sales range from 119.70 on 9 September to 1,147.62 on 22 September. The average daily total is 518.25.

Highest day22 September1,147.62 across 15 transactions

Interpretation: one month can describe variation, but it cannot establish seasonality or support dependable forecasting.

03

Customer contribution is distributed.

Customer 48 generated the highest recorded sales: 524.41 across four transactions. The top ten customers account for 26.3% of the month’s total.

Repeat within month74.4%of customers · 87.0% of recorded sales

Interpretation: this is repeat activity inside September, not a long-term retention rate.

04

Sales rise with transaction volume—but cautiously.

Daily transaction count and total daily sales have a Pearson correlation of r = 0.694, a moderately strong positive association.

Pearson correlation0.694daily total sales × daily transaction count

Interpretation: daily sales is the sum of transaction values, so transaction count is mechanically part of the outcome. This is not causal evidence.

07 / Statistical perspective

Point estimates need a measure of uncertainty.

A 10,000-sample percentile bootstrap estimates plausible ranges without assuming that the strongly skewed transaction values are normally distributed.

09 / Boundaries

What this project does not claim.

Time

One month cannot establish seasonality, trends or forecast reliability.

Business detail

No product, category, channel, location, cost or profit fields are available.

Customer history

Activity inside September cannot establish acquisition, churn or long-term retention.

Provenance

The original source, sampling method and currency were not documented in the earlier dataset.

Recommended next dataset

At least 12 months of transactions with product, category, store or channel, quantity, price, discount, cost and customer-acquisition fields—enabling seasonality, profitability, cohorts, segmentation and forecasting.
